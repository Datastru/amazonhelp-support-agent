# Golden set: how it was sampled and labeled

208 examples (within the 150-250 target), from `scripts/build_golden_set.py`
+ a hand-labeling pass.

## Sampling

1. Pool: every English customer-initiated thread for AmazonHelp with a root
   message over 15 characters (~28k threads after language filtering).
2. The baseline TF-IDF+LogisticRegression classifier (trained on the
   cluster-derived silver labels) predicts an intent and a confidence for
   every message in the pool. This is *only* used to stratify sampling -
   final labels come from a separate hand-labeling pass, not from trusting
   the classifier.
3. Per intent (8 intents x 26 candidates = 208 target before dedup): take the
   13 lowest-confidence predictions ("ambiguous" - genuinely hard/boundary
   cases, which a random sample under-represents) and the 13
   highest-confidence ("easy").
4. Near-duplicate removal: embed the 208 candidates, greedily drop anything
   with cosine similarity > 0.92 to an already-kept example (random order,
   seed 42). This step removed **zero** examples at this sample size - the
   per-intent quota sampling already produced enough diversity on its own.
   Said plainly here rather than claiming credit for a step that didn't end
   up doing anything.

Result: exactly 26 examples per intent (8 intents), 104 easy / 104 ambiguous
by the classifier's own *predicted* confidence. After hand-labeling the
*true* intent, the distribution shifted a lot:

| intent | stratified quota | true count after labeling |
|---|---|---|
| general_inquiry_or_other | 26 | 58 |
| delivery_problem | 26 | 27 |
| payment_or_giftcard_issue | 26 | 27 |
| device_or_app_tech_support | 26 | 26 |
| poor_support_experience | 26 | 23 |
| packaging_or_damage_complaint | 26 | 20 |
| refund_or_return_request | 26 | 16 |
| prime_membership_value | 26 | 11 |

`general_inquiry_or_other` more than doubled - a lot of messages the
baseline classifier confidently assigned to a specific intent turned out, on
reading, to be off-topic noise or praise. That's a real finding, not
sampling noise - the baseline is confidently wrong on a meaningful chunk of
traffic, concentrated in exactly the intent it has no training signal for
recognizing as "none of the above." 27 of 208 (13%) were hand-labeled as
needing escalation.

## Diversity, not just count

208 examples is not 208 unique complaints restated - see
`report/REPORT.md` section 4 for the actual root-issue count (distinct
complaints after grouping near-identical phrasings, done by hand during
labeling, not by an automated re-cluster).

## Labeling

Each of the 208 rows was hand-labeled (`scripts/_apply_golden_labels.py`
records the actual assignments) with:

- `true_intent`: one of the 8 taxonomy intents, from reading the tweet - not
  copied from the classifier's prediction. Several rows the classifier got
  "confidently wrong" turned up here, which is exactly the point of
  stratifying by its own confidence.
- `ideal_reply_direction`: a short phrase describing what a good reply should
  *do* (e.g. "apologize, ask for order # via DM to check status"), not a
  full scripted reply - a full reply is too subjective to use as ground
  truth, per the brief's own guidance.
- `escalate` + `escalate_reason`: a human judgment call using the same kind
  of reasoning as `pipeline/escalate.py`'s rules (safety, legal/PR,
  high-value ask, repeated unresolved contact, out-of-scope-for-a-bot), but
  applied by reading the tweet, not by running the regexes. Several labeled
  escalations are cases the actual rule-based policy's regexes would *miss*
  (abbreviated dollar amounts like "$15k", "illegal" without the phrase
  "legal action", repeated-contact phrasing outside the regex's patterns) -
  left that way on purpose, see decision log #16. Those show up as honest
  false negatives in the escalation eval, not hidden.

Labeling was done independently of the LLM classifier/drafter being
evaluated, by reading the text directly - not generated by the same model
under test, to keep the golden set an independent check rather than a
model grading itself.

## Human/judge agreement

A 35-example subset (seeded sample of the full eval run) was hand-scored on
the same 4-dimension rubric the LLM judge uses (grounded, tone, factual,
actionable), independently of the judge's own scores. Cohen's kappa between
the two is in `golden_set/human_judge_agreement.csv` and reported honestly in
`report/REPORT.md` section 5, including where it's weak.
